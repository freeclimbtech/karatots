About Us

About KARATOTS

KARATOTS is the children's branch of Karate Bristol, a dedicated class for 3 to 5 year olds, led by Sensei David and built on the same values that have shaped Karate Bristol for years.

Photo placeholder
Sensei David teaching a class
Sensei David

6th Dan. Renshi. Still teaching every class.

Sensei David founded Karate Bristol and holds the grade of 6th Dan with the title Renshi. His own path into karate was shaped by being bullied as a child. That experience still informs how he teaches today: with encouragement, not fear.

Over the years, he's helped hundreds of local students grow in body and mind, and KARATOTS reflects that same approach: no shouting, no pressure, just clear structure and a genuine interest in seeing children come out of their shell.

Karate Bristol → KARATOTS

The start of a longer journey

KARATOTS exists because the youngest children need their own space to begin, not a smaller version of an adult class, and not lumped in with six-to-eight-year-olds who are already finding their feet.

It's for ages 3 to 5 (and the odd quieter six-year-old who needs a gentler start), run by the same instructors, in the same club. When your child's ready to move on, they don't change clubs or start again from scratch, they simply join Karate Bristol's class for six-to-eight-year-olds, carrying everything they've already learned with them.

What We Teach

The values behind every class

Respect

Bowing to instructors, listening without interrupting, waiting their turn: small habits that build a bigger sense of respect for others.

Discipline

Following instructions, staying with a drill even when it's tricky, showing up and trying again next week.

Confidence

Small wins, repeated often: a new stance held a little longer, a louder call, a go at something they were nervous about.

Perseverance

Karate rewards children for trying again, not for getting it right first time. That's a lesson that reaches well beyond the dojo.

Focus

Watching, listening and copying a new movement is real concentration practice, dressed up as a game.

Community

Training in a small group of similarly aged children builds friendships and a sense of belonging from the first session.

Beyond the Basics

It's not really about fighting

Karate for young children is a framework for building physical and personal skills that show up everywhere else, not combat training.

Coordination

Moving arms, legs and attention together.

Balance

Stability that supports everyday movement.

Fitness

Stamina and strength, built through play.

Confidence

Trying, in front of others, without fear.

Concentration

Watching, listening, and following through.

Discipline

Sticking with something, even when it's hard.

Respect

For instructors, classmates, and themselves.

Perseverance

Trying again after getting it wrong.
